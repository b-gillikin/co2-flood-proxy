# Weekly literature triage: catchment response variability

Checked 2026-10-16 against the publisher article and the active Limburg
analogue/storm-response designs. This is supporting interpretation, not a new
model arm or chapter result.

[Yan, Sharif and Ameli (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-026-04079-6)
analyse about 1.7 million runoff-producing events across 4,838 catchments.
They report substantial event-to-event variation in catchment hydrologic
function even within similar broad water-input and antecedent-soil-moisture
categories. This supports treating different responses among Limburg episodes
as a substantive interpretation problem rather than assuming that rainfall
similarity ensures flow similarity.

Their global transfer-function classes, seasonal split and event definitions
differ from Limburg's rainfall-defined episodes and local gauge comparisons.
The reported percentages are not estimates for the six Limburg tributaries;
the paper does not identify the mechanism behind any local divergence or
validate a new predictor. Keep the existing controls and uncertainty rules.

The 2026-10-16 weekly digest also said German LANUK records had not been
pooled. That statement is stale. The current analysis includes a pooled
Dutch/German discharge-only comparison; German rainfall/weather inputs are
still unavailable for the corresponding R0/R3 comparison.
