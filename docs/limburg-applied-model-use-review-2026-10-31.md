# Applied uses of the held models: task fit and practical value

31 October 2026. Internal research aid; Brian writes manuscript prose. Literature/documentation review and proposed next tests, not new empirical findings. No active queue, source contract, RQ, extraction or fitting was changed.

## Direction agreed with Brian

Test models in roles supported by their actual design and applications. Different models may contribute at different points in a pipeline. A useful contribution may be higher accuracy, comparable accuracy at lower cost, greater label/data efficiency, more transferable outputs, or simpler reuse. A missing feed or named operator decision is **not a prerequisite**. FEWS/DRR relevance guides the work without imposing a deployment project.

The completed discharge experiments remain valid tests of their specified frozen-feature corrections. They are adaptations from generic EO representations to discharge, not comprehensive tests of each model's native downstream use. Correct input normalization and pooling do not settle task fit. Conversely, weak discharge increments do not prove the adapters are defective.

Two already-held supporting models have different roles: **TTM** is a time-series foundation model and belongs in a permitted-history temporal prediction task; **AlphaEarth** is an annual landscape representation and belongs in spatial characterization/transfer, not event-hour flood detection. Keep these roles separate rather than force all models into every task.

## A useful separation of empirical questions

1. **Can it estimate the observable property?** Examples: water/non-water, event water versus persistent water, or an independently labeled land-surface class. Use a spatial decoder, linear probe or fine-tuning route supported by that checkpoint. Score against labels at the acquisition time. This is where the model's native application can be tested directly.
2. **Does that estimate help explain or predict the river response?** Only then test the derived map/state/landscape information in the hydrological assessment. A model can succeed at mapping while adding little to discharge; that locates its contribution rather than declaring it useless.
3. **What does it cost to achieve that performance?** Count imagery preparation, labels, model loading, extraction, adaptation and repeated application. Similar scores can still be useful with less labeling, memory, runtime or data dependence. A model can also be more expensive and offer no advantage.

These questions can share one applied chapter narrative, but they need not become three full new studies. Do not create a ten-model × every-task experiment. Match candidate to task, then expand comparisons when they answer the same empirical question.

## Comparisons that would be informative

- **Direct spatial task:** frozen encoder plus a simple trained head versus direct spectral/radar features with a comparably tuned predictor. Include an ordinary trained segmentation model when a spatial decoder is central. If claiming pretraining benefit, compare with a suitable from-scratch counterpart; do not attribute architecture gains automatically to pretraining.
- **Label efficiency:** hold the evaluation locations/events fixed; vary training-label availability using nested, reproducible subsets. Plot accuracy against label count and label-acquisition effort. Repeated label subsets are not independent flood events.
- **Transfer:** evaluate on held events/locations; separate sensor/product changes from geographic transfer. Pixel-random splits across one scene cannot establish new-event generalization.
- **Practical efficiency:** compare accuracy–resource trade-offs on the same hardware, image area and batch conditions. Track peak memory and full latency, not just cached-head speed. Report cold start, one-off extraction and amortized reuse separately. Existing interrupted/multi-worker elapsed-time logs are engineering records, not a fair model-speed benchmark.
- **Equal performance:** an interval crossing zero is not equivalence. Any claim of practically comparable accuracy needs an application-relevant tolerance justified before the comparison; otherwise report the accuracy/cost trade-off and uncertainty without declaring equivalence.
- **Physical interpretation:** a flood map is not discharge or depth. Rainfall plus mapped surface water is not a hydraulic model. Translation between stages is a hypothesis to test, not an automatic consequence of chaining software.

## Concrete local evidence leads and what is still unknown

Copernicus EMS [EMSR520](https://mapping.emergency.copernicus.eu/activations/EMSR520/) documents the July 2021 Netherlands/Meuse flood-mapping activation. [EMSR517](https://mapping.emergency.copernicus.eu/activations/EMSR517/) covers western Germany. These are real mapping-response contexts and candidate reference products, **not yet verified labels over our eight tributary catchments**. Check each AOI geometry, delineation acquisition time, input sensor, mapping confidence and delivery timestamp before using it. The Netherlands activation began late on 15 July; its outputs cannot be relabeled as observations available at an earlier peak.

The held local flood register contains 15 records mixing threshold episodes, coarse documentary statements and impact evidence. It is not a pixel-level inundation label set. Current H3 applied retrospectively does not label a satellite pixel as flooded. The existing imagery was assembled primarily as antecedent context; a suitable during-event scene and a temporally compatible reference must be inventoried explicitly. Missing mapped water may mean unobserved, outside AOI or undetectable—not dry.

Published open benchmarks provide an acquisition path for testing the intended task without claiming local validation. Their train/test events and potential pretraining overlap must be retained. Kuro Siwo is a particularly direct lead because Copernicus-FM itself specifies the flood-change benchmark; TerraMind and Prithvi have documented mapping adaptations. None is selected solely because our earlier discharge scores were unfavorable.
