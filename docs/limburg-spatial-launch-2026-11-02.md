## Runtime and input reconciliation

The original Galileo spatial cache is preserved. All 446 chips were regenerated
under the current source version; every feature file is byte-identical to its
original. The launch uses `limburg_applied_galileo_spatial_v2` and its new source
record. Bounded RAM caches now avoid reopening whole radar/label rasters for
every 64px subpatch and every epoch. A real-raster regression test confirms
identical normalized inputs, valid labels, subpatches and acquisition months.
Each process can retain at most 446 raw chips and 446 frozen feature arrays,
roughly2GiB together; this is an engineering input cache, not a learned change.

Copernicus's native Apple GPU attempt failed in the published float64 Fourier
calculation. Its frozen encoder now performs the exact author calculations on
CPU; its unchanged trainable neck/UPerNet/auxiliary head runs on MPS. The same
exact adaptive-pooling/layout compatibility used for Prithvi is applied to the
Copernicus decoder's parameter-free pools/BatchNorm layout, including SyncBN.
No vendor source, library binary, input bands, precision of the author's Fourier
calculation, model parameters or task target was substituted.

All 5,000 frozen before/after spatial feature differences are cached at
`data/processed/limburg_copernicus_spatial_v1`: four 768-channel14x14 maps per
record, float32,11.22GiB. Extraction took894.7s. These retain spatial structure;
they are not catchment-pooled embeddings. Labels never enter the encoder.
Three independently recomputed training records and their decoder outputs were
bit-identical with/without the cache. Twelve cached-head training batches had
finite gradients; steady median time was0.187s/batch under engineering checks.
This excludes loader/validation/checkpoint cost and is not a whole-grid ETA.
All four Copernicus learner profiles passed on MPS. The cold encoder extraction
cost is included separately in manifests; it must not vanish from efficiency
claims or be multiplied once per fitted head.

## Storage and recovery

Keep 50GiB free plus 5GiB launch headroom. Every completed job is bundled with its
logs, candidates, selected test predictions, local maps and provenance. Script207
uploads additively to private Azure Blob, downloads the entire bundle, checks its
archive SHA256 and every member, and writes a recovery receipt. Only then may
script214 remove that job's inactive `.pt` checkpoint files. All score/provenance
records and prediction maps stay local; archived checkpoint sizes remain in the
readout. A local-application failure retains checkpoints for follow-up. Failed
uploads never authorize removal. Temporary bundle/readback copies are removed
only after verification. Other chapters' data are untouched. No cloud VM/GPU
is provisioned.

The source snapshot, launch manifest, completed review, cache indexes and runtime
receipts are separately preserved. All raw/model sources already have their
existing recovery paths; regenerable caches are not another mandatory raw-data
acquisition. Full-grid throughput must be estimated from the initial complete
fits including validation/I/O; this is substantial work, not an overnight promise.
