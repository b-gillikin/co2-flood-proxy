- **René's held clarification**: fixed UTC+1 timestamps and genuine zero discharge remain the source semantics. A simulated outage must not change those facts. Historical final records are verification evidence, not an archived record of what an operator actually received.

Accordingly, the study simulates **counterfactual availability of valid station information**. Its practical motivation is credible, but neither agency's empirical latency distribution or frequency of complete outages has been estimated. No new request is needed to perform the counterfactual study; stronger real-time frequency or as-issued claims would need different evidence.

## Changes justified by the review

Adopt the simpler monitoring-resilience RQ. Distinguish delivery delay from permanent loss through repeated issue times, because equal information at one instant cannot yield different assessments merely from different scenario names. Preserve the full census while making the already-observed-rainfall subset the primary clock. Preserve local historical calibration and use same-gauge native-unit trajectories so hidden Q/p99 cannot anchor a forecast. Keep satellite age, seasonal information, donor-archive loss and product-release uncertainty visible.

Weekly context is physically closer to antecedent state than a completed monthly snapshot, but it has smaller held support; retain both declared roles rather than searching for a winning view. Full-history TTM vectors are not valid sparse-Q inputs. All old full-information, flood-case and Meuse results remain reference evidence. Nothing in the reviewed sources establishes that our ten EO encoders will improve outage assessments, operational usefulness, or a unique worldwide novelty claim. That is precisely why the new comparison is worth testing rather than announcing as a finding.
