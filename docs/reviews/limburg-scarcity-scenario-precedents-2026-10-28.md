## Recommended matrix for the next executable plan

These are recommendations grounded in the above designs, not completed simulations or claims that the exact numbers are universal standards.

| Axis | Recommended starting levels | Attribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scattered missing discharge inputs | **0, 25, 50, 75, 100% withheld** | Grid from Nearing; scattered-pattern comparator supported by Mbuvha. The pointwise generator is a different pattern from Nearing's clustered generator. |
| Clustered missing discharge inputs | Same fractions; **6, 12, 24h mean missing-run durations** initially | Fraction and two-state mechanism from Nearing; hourly duration choices are our adaptation, supported by Ren and the Oder study. Include longer 48/72h cases as declared prolonged-gap support if warranted by the history windows. |
| Observation frequency | **1, 3, 6, 12, 24h** | 6/12/24 from Rakovec; 1h reference and 3h intermediate adapted for the held hourly, smaller-tributary task. These are reporting intervals, not future forecast horizons. |
| Tributary-gauge coverage | Retain **8, 6, 4, 2, 0** observing gauges | Quarter-step removal is literature-backed; applying it to this eight-gauge network and the zero-gauge endpoint is our adaptation. Retain all target outcomes. |
| Historical donor archive | Full earlier archive versus caps of **3, 6, 9 years** | Length levels follow Gauch; rolling earlier-donor caps are our adaptation, not their fixed-start training experiment. Report actual available history and avoid deleting early cases merely because nine years are unavailable. |
| Rainfall scarcity | Separate temporal coarsening/input-loss comparison | Precedent supports the dimension; exact radar coarsening and availability rules still need to match held products and the rainfall-based assessment clock. Do not pretend rain-station removal directly describes removing pixels from gauge-adjusted radar. |

Keep an optional **10%** mild-missingness check, motivated by Zhang/Post and Mbuvha, if the quarter-step grid proves too coarse at the mild end. Choose this before inspecting scenario performance, not to obtain a more favorable EO result.

Withholding percentages describe the additional simulated loss. Original natural gaps remain missing. Record the nominal mask fraction, the fraction of originally observed samples newly hidden and the resulting total missingness, so a simulated “0%” condition is not misrepresented as a perfect record. At 100%, verification outcomes and historical response labels remain held: it is the specified input channel that disappears, not the evidence used to check assessments.

Do not form the full Cartesian product. First explain individual axes; then specify a few recognizable combined situations. Retain the entire eligible assessment census. Repeated mask realizations represent monitoring uncertainty, not sampling a smaller set of events.

## Implementation choices we still own

1. **Pattern coherence and replication.** Generate masks on continuous source timelines, reuse them across methods and issue times, preserve genuine zeros, and record seeds. Start with **100 common mask realizations**, following Zhang/Post's replication precedent; benchmark/cache computation rather than reduce the event census. Scattered versus blocked masking can be compared at equal missing fraction. Nested severities are useful when compatible with the chosen run-duration mechanism; report actual fractions and durations rather than force impossible combinations. At 0/100% missingness use explicit endpoints, not a singular transition formula.
2. **Density versus age.** Keep latest-reading age fixed for a controlled density contrast. Also characterize fixed reporting schedules/phases when evaluating realistic combined density-plus-age effects. A lower reporting frequency naturally changes age; label the estimand instead of claiming pure density. Recompute features from retained observations. The existing Q(s), Q(s−6h), Q(s−24h) signature can be unchanged by three/six-hour thinning, so it cannot alone establish the usefulness of dense histories.
3. **Which gauges remain.** The same number of stations can leave very different information. With eight tributary response gauges, the proposed retained sizes have **128 total subsets**: 1 + 28 + 70 + 28 + 1. Enumerating that mask inventory is a practical alternative to handpicking favorable networks; it is not 128 independent river systems. Keep the two held Maas context stations as a separately identified network component, and inspect upstream/downstream, country and target-gauge-retained versus target-gauge-removed conditions. Exact context features and runtime belong in the executable plan. The current local-only matcher does not become network-aware simply because gauge files are removed.
4. **Timing and mechanism realism.** Ordinary random missingness, multi-hour gaps and failures associated with high water are different scenarios. A separately labelled rising/high-water stress profile can supplement neutral masks. It must not leak a hidden flood label into issue scheduling or be claimed to estimate real outage frequency. Provider logs/descriptions could calibrate durations and dependence later; they are not required to begin controlled simulations.
5. **Fair conventional treatment.** Compare EO with capable conventional/missing-data treatments on the same retained data and donor support. Each reconstruction may use only readings available by issue. A gap can have a legal post-gap endpoint if that endpoint is already in the observed prefix; an endpoint after issue is hidden. Retain full verification records and whole-storm/year inference. Missing EO falls back explicitly. Historical-data scarcity, contemporary network scarcity and missing input samples remain distinct.

Our contribution would be the controlled information-loss evaluation and any demonstrated value of frozen EO context, rather than inventing missing-data percentages. This literature supports a concrete matrix; it does not guarantee positive EO findings or prove a globally unique novelty claim.
