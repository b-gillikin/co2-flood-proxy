## Assessment

The evidence supports event comparison with explicit applicability and failure
information. The selected historical events are closer in individual response
shape than the eligible archive in general. A modest part of that increment is
specific to ordered TTM rather than the matched raw-history method. However,
matching individual precedents and averaging them into one accurate response
estimate are different tasks. Generic historical averaging is a stronger
reference than flat persistence, especially for duration.

This distinction sharpens the existing question; it does not replace it with
forecasting or introduce a new model roster. The reference calculation is the
unranked eligible archive, weighted equally by onset-linked group and equally
among each group's members. It is an exploratory comparison introduced to
check whether apparent information merely repeats typical recession. It has
no fitted parameters or learned weights and uses exactly each query's existing
finished prior-response pool. Query outcomes only verify it.

Q is normalized by each event's own full-era p99. Shape additionally divides
each observed response by its own maximum, for verification only. The generic
shape measure is the group-balanced average query-to-archive-event shape
mismatch, not shape error of the archive's mean curve. The generic duration,
late level and mean peak estimates average those descriptors over the same
weighted archive. No best reference or donor count is selected after seeing
which gives a favorable headline.

- Selected TTM donors reduce mean individual shape mismatch by about **14.3%**
  relative to the eligible archive with all rivers, and **13.6%** with other
  rivers. Raw + state already achieves about 12.3% in both. The unique TTM
  increments over raw remain the earlier approximately 2.2% / 1.5%.
- Cross-river TTM shape minus archive mismatch is -0.03207, paired query-group
  interval [-0.03604, -0.02806]. All-river, year-block and quality checks also
  support the direction. This supports response-shape specificity relative to
  available precedents; it does not establish successful operator understanding.
- The generic archive has lower duration error. TTM minus archive is +0.879h
  all-river [0.443, 1.329], and +1.073h cross-river [0.644, 1.505]; year-block
  checks support both. Duration information beyond flat persistence was partly
  typical historical recession, not a demonstrated retrieval advantage.
- Generic averaging also has lower whole-curve error in point summaries.
  Cross-river TTM minus archive is +0.01927, group interval [0.00709, 0.03190],
  but its year interval includes zero. All-river intervals include zero. Keep
  this sensitivity rather than declare a universal aggregate-curve winner.
- TTM improves all-river final-24h level relative to the archive by 0.01709,
  group interval [-0.02794, -0.00609]; the year check supports it. The
  cross-river point increment is favorable but its year interval includes zero.
- Mean-peak contrasts against the archive include zero. Current cutoff Q also
  remains a strong peak reference. Historical shape relevance does not by
  itself identify severity, peak time or local warning-threshold crossings.

For cross-river TTM, 644 of 845 queries have lower mean individual shape
mismatch than the archive; 373 have lower mean-curve level error, 340 lower
duration error, and 468 lower final-24h level error. These are numeric
comparisons. They are not counts of useful assessments or independent human
relevance verdicts. The all-river shape count is 659 of 845.

Intervals use the existing 9,999 query-group/year resamples, seed 20261013,
conditional on the historical archive. They do not cover observation,
reused-donor, pretraining or exploratory multiple-comparison uncertainty.
The generic archive is a historical reference, not an as-issued forecast.

## Chapter claims and policy implications

| Proposed claim or implication | Evidence status |
|---|---|
| Basin-constrained historical retrieval selects closer response-shape precedents across these tributaries. | Supported relative to the specified eligible archive; retrospective and conditional on this cohort. |
| Frozen ordered TTM contributes a modest increment over equally state-informed raw histories. | Supported for shape, and all-river late level; magnitude/duration and several cross-river extensions remain uncertain. |
| The retrieval system reliably predicts event continuation better than ordinary historical averaging. | Unsupported; the generic archive is stronger for duration and often the mean trajectory. |
| The returned cases add readable examples of possible continuation and counterexamples to a single confident narrative. | Supported by the documented AI-assisted cases and numeric specificity; independent human usefulness has not been evaluated. |
| Small donor disagreement means an assessment is dependable. | Unsupported; actual reviewed low-spread failures contradict that inference. |
| Dutch and German warning regimes or normalized discharge peaks are interchangeable. | Unsupported. Wurm/Worm physical identity, provider provenance and local rules remain distinct information. |
| The method improves FEWS decisions, warning lead time, preparedness or losses. | Not tested. These are possible future uses, not a chapter result. |

**Policy inference from the evidence:** the plausible contribution is an
inspectable historical casebook for flood analysts, training or post-event
review. An assessment could present the query history, identified prior events,
individual subsequent paths, a generic reference, competing method assessments
and measurement caveats. Its value would be making the grounds and limits of a
comparison visible across differently documented gauge records.

Any later operational trial should assess whether that comparison helps people
recognize relevant precedents and mistaken transfer, rather than assume a
small numerical shape improvement changes a warning decision. The present
chapter does not need interviews, deployment, a dashboard or an operational
trial to report its retrospective evidence. Those are future evaluation options.
Do not translate own-era p99 into an official warning phase, infer inundation
from Q alone, or treat current finalized records as information known in real
time. Brian can use these points to frame recommendations without an
unsupported claim of current operational readiness.

This pass leaves the RQ and models intact. It gives a firmer account of what
“useful analogues” means in the existing evidence, including counterexamples;
it does not make a model's average victory a condition for the chapter to exist.
