# Weekly literature: pooled versus within-catchment skill

[Ford et al. (2026), What do machine learning models learn about floods?](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-2026-5890)
is retained as a **preprint under review**, not a peer-reviewed result. The
authors compare event-based RF and continuous LSTM models on flood magnitudes
across 518 British catchments. Pooled predictive skill substantially exceeds
within-catchment skill; similar predictive performance can also coexist with
different feature-importance structures.

For our current analogue/embedding study, the relevant question is whether
representations distinguish useful events rather than primarily separating
river identities or characteristic magnitudes. The existing matched-event,
matched-donor, within-/cross-river and country comparisons help examine that
distinction. The paper does not establish a defect in those comparisons or
demonstrate that any Limburg embedding succeeds or fails. Its numerical effects
do not transfer directly to this small, partly nested local cohort.

Keep it as evaluation and interpretation literature. Do not introduce RF/LSTM
training, feature attribution or new data requirements. Chapter 0 retains the
same library record as a methods source; formal review eligibility is undecided.
